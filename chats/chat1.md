## User

Project: Nexa — Premium Gadget Store Visual Design
Style direction: Premium and quietly confident, in the spirit of Apple Store — but this must NOT look like a generic AI-generated design. Avoid the common tells: no warm cream background with terracotta accents, no near-black background with a single neon accent, no identical rounded product cards with the same soft grey shadow on every one, no tracked-out all-caps eyebrow labels, no middle-dot meta strings, no arrow (→) tacked onto buttons or links. The goal is a genuinely distinctive visual identity that couldn't be mistaken for a template — something built specifically around gadgets/phones/tech, not a generic "premium store" skin.
How to get there:
Ground the design in the actual subject matter — phones, laptops, headsets, power banks — and let that vocabulary (materials, precision, ports, edges, screens, metal/glass surfaces) inform layout and detail choices, not just product photos on a white background
Pick one deliberate, opinionated hero treatment (not the default big-headline-plus-gradient) — something that feels specific to a gadget store, not any premium e-commerce brand
Choose typefaces deliberately — not the obvious "clean tech sans" default — and set a real type scale with intentional weight/spacing choices
Spend visual boldness in exactly one place (e.g. the hero, or the product gallery interaction) and keep everything else disciplined and quiet
Use structural devices (dividers, labels, spacing) only where they encode real information, not as decoration
Motion should be restrained: one deliberate signature moment (e.g. product image transition) rather than fade-up animations on every section/card
Color palette:
Primary background: near-white or very light neutral
Text: near-black, but not a generic flat black — pick a deliberate tint
One restrained accent color, chosen specifically for this brand (avoid default terracotta/orange or acid-green/neon choices) — something that could become recognizably "Nexa's" color
Let product photography carry visual interest; color should support, not compete
Typography:
A deliberate, specific typeface pairing (not the default "SF Pro-style" safe choice) — something with real character that still reads premium and legible
Clear price hierarchy through weight and scale, not color or size gimmicks
Avoid single-word accent styling in headlines, all-caps labels, or unnecessary eyebrow text above sections
Key screens to design:
Home screen — hero + category navigation + featured grid
Category/listing screen with filters
Product detail screen with gallery and variant selector
Cart drawer/page
Wishlist screen
Checkout screen
UI elements:
Product cards/tiles should feel considered, not identical rounded-corner-plus-shadow defaults — vary treatment where it serves hierarchy (e.g. featured vs. standard listings)
Filter/sort controls with a distinctive but usable interaction style (not default pill buttons)
One signature interaction moment on the product page (gallery, variant swap, or add-to-cart) that feels crafted, not templated
Sticky mobile "Add to cart" bar, styled to match the brand's specific character
Mood references: Apple.com / Apple Store as a quality bar (craft, restraint, confidence) — not as a style to copy. The end result should feel like its own brand, not an Apple clone.
